it is easy to make a portfolio look like every other portfolio: a grid of case studies, a dark hero, a cursor that trails behind your mouse. i wanted this one to sound like me instead.

the version you are reading is one column of text at one size. links are grey chips. color only shows up where it carries meaning, like a brand you hover or a contribution graph. everything else is spacing.

## what i left out

- a hero image. the first sentence already says who i am.
- a dark mode. one theme, tuned carefully, beats two that are almost right.
- animation on scroll. text that waits for you before it appears is text you cannot skim.

leaving these out made a lot of later decisions easy. every page uses the same column, so i never argue with myself about layout. every link looks the same, so you never have to guess what is clickable. when something moves, it is because something changed, and i wrote about [how i decide that](/notes/motion-should-explain-something) in another note.

> the constraint was never the point. it just made the details easier to see.

the details are where the time went. the back button sits in the margin so titles line up with the text below them. dates use tabular figures so a column of them lines up. chips have a pressed state, so a tap feels like it landed.

if a page here ever needs more than this, it probably needs fewer words, not more design.
